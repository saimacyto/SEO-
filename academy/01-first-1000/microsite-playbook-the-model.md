# The Microsite Model: A Small Site That Rings a Phone

That is the whole thing. You build a site targeting one service in one town, get it ranking,
and when someone in that town needs that service, they call the number on it. You then either
hand those calls to a local business for a monthly fee, or charge per lead.

It is not a brochure site and it is not a blog. Nobody is going to admire it. It gets judged by
the lead flow, not the design.

## What it actually looks like

- One primary service.
- A couple of extra service pages so it reads like a real business rather than a single
  landing page.
- One to two dozen pages covering the towns and neighborhoods around the target area.

That lands most first sites around **50 to 80 pages**. Sounds like a lot until you see the
Builder produce it in an afternoon.

The domain does real work here: an exact-match domain like `plumbershouston.com` tells Google
what the site is before it reads a word of content — which is why picking the name gets its
own dedicated step (Step 2).

## Why a small site beats a big one

You're not trying to outrank national companies for the big obvious keyword. You're picking up
the long tail underneath it — specific searches nobody optimized for, in one geography, where
the person searching is ready to call someone right now.

A lead like that is worth real money to a local contractor — one job often pays for the site
many times over. That's the economics the whole method rests on.

Because each site is cheap, the portfolio compounds: one member built 78 of these in six
months. Fifty sites at eighty pages each is four thousand chances to be the page someone lands
on.

## "Isn't this spam?"

A fair question that comes up every time.

- The sites do **not** link to each other — this is not a private blog network.
- Each one is a real site about a real service in a real place, with true information on it.
- What makes it work: the search results page has more than one slot, and most local searches
  have nobody serious competing for the specific phrases being targeted.

**The line worth holding:** every page should say something true. If a page could not survive a
local reading it, it should not go up.
