# Niche Decision: Septic — Dripping Springs, TX

**Status:** **COMMITTED** — domain purchased: `septicdrippingsprings.com`

## Why this town/niche

- **100% septic-dependent:** Dripping Springs has no municipal sewer system at all; every
  property runs on an individual septic system (Hays County OSSF permitting, TCEQ Chapter 285).
- **High ticket, confirmed:** Edwards Plateau limestone geology (only 6-18 inches of topsoil)
  forces 80-90% of properties into engineered aerobic/alternative systems costing
  **$15,000-$30,000** — well above the $5k target.
- **Income supports the ticket:** median household income **$109,364** — well above national
  median, genuinely can afford the ticket size.
- **Regulatory moat:** permit required on every lot regardless of acreage; aerobic systems
  require a continuous *licensed* maintenance contract (no homeowner self-maintenance) — hard
  for a low-effort competitor or national chain to templatize.
- **No permit-freeze risk found** (unlike well drilling in the same town — see below) — septic
  permitting through Hays County appears to be operating normally, no drought-emergency
  restriction found for septic specifically.

## Comparison: why septic over well drilling in this same town

Well drilling in Dripping Springs has the same ticket upside ($12k-24k, limestone-driven
drilling costs) and the same income support, but the Hays Trinity Groundwater Conservation
District is currently in **Emergency Drought Stage** and not accepting new permits for
production/non-exempt well construction (ordinary small-scale domestic wells are often exempt
under Texas groundwater law, but this needs verification before committing capital there).
Septic carries no equivalent regulatory freeze found — cleaner pick. Well drilling stays a
possible secondary microsite for this town, pending that exemption question being resolved.

## Domain check (Step 2 rules)

`septicdrippingsprings.com` — passes all five rules: `.com`, exact match (not branded), one
intent (septic) + one geography (Dripping Springs), matches `[service][location].com`. Keeping
the service term broad ("septic" rather than "septic installation") is a deliberate choice —
it lets the site rank across the whole vertical (installation, repair, pumping, inspection)
rather than narrowing to installation-only searches.
