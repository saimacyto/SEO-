# YouTube Metric Benchmarks

Healthy ranges for grading each metric. These are general benchmarks across most niches. Niche, audience size, and video length shift them, so always read in context, never in isolation. When in doubt, grade against the *combination* of metrics, not one number.

## 1. CTR (impressions click-through rate)

What share of people who saw the thumbnail clicked it. The clearest signal of packaging (title + thumbnail) quality.

| CTR | Grade | Read |
|---|---|---|
| < 2% | 🔴 | Packaging is losing. Thumbnail or title isn't earning the click. |
| 2-4% | 🟡 | Below average for most niches. Room to sharpen. |
| 4-6% | 🟢 | Healthy. The packaging is doing its job. |
| 6-8% | 🟢 | Strong. Click-worthy packaging. |
| 8-12% | 🟢🟢 | Excellent. This is a packaging win to study and reuse. |
| > 12% | ⚠️ | Either elite packaging, a small/loyal sample, or mostly subscriber traffic (subs click at higher rates). Check impressions volume before celebrating. |

**Caveats:**
- CTR *falls* as impressions scale. A 10% CTR on 50K impressions and a 7% CTR on 900K impressions can mean the second video is the bigger win - it earned far more total clicks while still converting well at scale.
- Big channels and heavily-subscribed videos show lower CTR because the subscriber feed inflates impressions with low-intent views.
- CTR only matters paired with impressions. High CTR + tiny impressions = good packaging YouTube isn't promoting yet.

## 2. Average percentage viewed (APV) - length-adjusted

The single most misread metric. A % viewed is meaningless without the video length. Short videos post high percentages easily; long videos can't, and shouldn't be judged the same way.

| Video length | 🔴 Weak | 🟡 OK | 🟢 Good | 🟢🟢 Great |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Under 8 min | < 20% | 20-30% | 30-40% | 40%+ |
| 8-15 min | < 18% | 18-25% | 25-35% | 35%+ |
| 15+ min | < 15% | 15-22% | 22-30% | 30%+ |

**Always convert APV to AVD before grading.** APV × length = AVD. A 22% APV on a 21-minute video is a 4:45 AVD, which is strong watch-time fuel even though 22% "sounds low." Long videos with moderate APV often beat short videos with high APV on total watch time, and watch time is what the algorithm rewards.

---

## 3. Average view duration (AVD)

Absolute minutes watched per view. This is the real watch-time engine and the thing YouTube's suggested/browse systems actually optimize toward.

| AVD | Grade (for long-form 10min+ talking-head / education / AI / business) |
|---|---|
| < 2:30 | 🔴 Thin. People aren't staying. |
| 2:30-4:00 | 🟡 Acceptable. |
| 4:00-6:00 | 🟢 Solid. Good watch-time fuel. |
| 6:00+ | 🟢🟢 Strong. This is what gets pushed. |

Compare to the niche and to the creator's own other videos when possible. A 4:45 AVD on a 21-min video is a healthy number that gives YouTube a lot of watch time to work with.

## 4. 30-second retention

The share still watching at ~30s. The hook's report card. The biggest single lever on a video's reach, because YouTube decides fast whether to keep promoting.

| 30s retention | Grade |
|---|---|
| < 50% | 🔴 The hook is leaking. Rework the open. |
| 50-65% | 🟡 OK, not special. |
| 65-75% | 🟢 Good hook. The promise landed. |
| 75%+ | 🟢🟢 Elite open. Reuse this hook structure. |

If YouTube Studio shows a callout like "X% are still watching at 0:30, which is above typical," treat "above typical" as a 🟢 regardless of the raw number, and note the structure that earned it.

---

## 5. Retention curve shape

The shape tells you more than any single number. Read it left to right against the gray "typical retention" band.

- **Initial cliff (0-30s):** Every video drops here. You want it *shallow*. A steep early cliff = hook/promise mismatch with the packaging.
- **Blue line above the gray band:** You're beating the typical video of this length. Good. Whatever you're doing, keep it.
- **Blue line below the gray band:** You're underperforming for a video this length. YouTube sees this and throttles distribution. Find what's dragging it.
- **A cliff mid-video (sharp drop):** Something lost them. Map it to the runtime: common culprits are sponsor reads, "let me recap," slow setup, a tangent, or an over-long intro to a section. Name the likely cause from its position.
- **A swell (line goes UP):** A re-watched moment, a loop, or a chapter people skipped back to. Worth studying - that's content that worked.
- **A long flat tail to the end:** The people who stayed were locked in. Even at low %, a flat tail is a good sign for the algorithm and for end-screen clicks.

## 6. Subscriber conversion

Subscribers gained ÷ views on the video. How well the content converts a viewer into a follower.

| Subs ÷ views | Grade |
|---|---|
| < 0.5% | 🔴 Low. The content isn't making people want more. |
| 0.5-1% | 🟡 Normal. |
| 1-2% | 🟢 Strong. The video earns trust. |
| 2%+ | 🟢🟢 Exceptional. This video is a subscriber machine - make more like it. |

Example: 3,064 subs on 91,739 views = 3.3%, which is exceptional and signals the topic/format deeply resonated.

---

## 7. RPM / revenue

RPM = estimated revenue ÷ (views ÷ 1000). Earnings per thousand views. Heavily dependent on niche, audience geography, and how many ads ran.

| Niche | Rough RPM range |
|---|---|
| Finance / business / B2B / AI tools | $6-25 |
| Tech / software / education | $4-12 |
| General / lifestyle / entertainment | $1-5 |

Note: RPM (what the creator keeps per 1K views) is lower than CPM (what advertisers pay) because of YouTube's cut and unmonetized views. A US/UK-heavy audience pays far more than an India/SEA-heavy one, so weak RPM on a high-view video is often a geography story, not a content problem. Check demographics before blaming the content.

---

## 8. Impressions

How many times YouTube showed the thumbnail. A measure of how hard YouTube is promoting the video.

- **High and climbing:** YouTube is testing it widely. The system likes the early signals.
- **High impressions + low CTR:** Packaging miss. YouTube gave it reach; the thumbnail/title didn't convert. Fixable and high-leverage.
- **High impressions + high CTR + rising views:** A scaling winner. Don't touch the packaging; make the sequel.
- **Low impressions:** YouTube isn't promoting it yet, usually because early retention or CTR didn't clear the bar. The problem is upstream (hook or packaging), not reach.

---

## 9. Views trajectory

The shape of the views-over-time line in the views-by-content chart.

- **Rising / climbing line:** Browse and suggested are picking it up. This is the evergreen/viral signal. Keep promoting, make related content now while it's hot.
- **Sharp peak then decline:** A front-loaded push (notification spike, a Short feeding it, an external blast) that's now fading. Normal, but it won't sustain on its own - it needs browse/suggested to take over.
- **Flat then suddenly rising (sleeper):** YouTube found an audience late. Often the best kind - it means evergreen search/suggested traction. Lean in.
- **Flat and low throughout:** Never got distribution. Upstream problem (packaging or early retention).

---

## 10. Combination reads (the important part)

Single metrics lie. Combinations tell the truth. Use these to write the verdict.

| Pattern | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| High CTR + high APV/AVD + rising views | Genuine winner. Make the sequel before the trend cools. |
| High CTR + low retention | Packaging oversold the content. Great click, weak payoff. Fix the body so it delivers the thumbnail's promise. |
| Low CTR + high retention | The content is great but the packaging is capping reach. The people who click stay; not enough click. Rework title/thumbnail. |
| High impressions + low CTR | YouTube gave reach, packaging didn't convert. Highest-leverage fix on the channel. |
| High APV but low AVD | Video is just short. Fine, but limited watch-time ceiling. Consider longer formats for the same topic. |
| Low APV but high AVD | Long video, moderate %, lots of total watch time. Often a quiet winner. Don't panic about the %. |
| High sub conversion + modest views | The format resonates hard with the people who find it. Distribution is the bottleneck, not the content. Push packaging + more of this topic. |
| Strong everything + flat trajectory | Got the audience it'll get and stopped. Either the niche is small or it needs an external push to re-trigger browse. |

**The chain to always reason through:** packaging earns the click (CTR) → hook earns the first 30s → retention earns the watch time (AVD) → watch time + satisfaction earns distribution (suggested/browse) → distribution earns views, subs, revenue. Find the weakest link in *that specific video's* chain. That's the headline of the audit.
